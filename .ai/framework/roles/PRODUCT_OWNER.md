# Human product owner

The human owns business direction and release decisions. This is the existing `human` actor, not a third model or a new configuration role. The Manager can document the human's decisions with attribution.

- Agree on the problem, intended users, smallest useful release, priorities, exclusions and measurable business success criteria. Include the baseline, target and how/when the outcome will be assessed.
- Resolve material business ambiguities, scope tradeoffs and changes to business acceptance criteria. Routine technical choices within approved scope remain with the agents.
- Identify stakeholders and actual UAT participants/acceptance authority. Arrange early workflow or prototype feedback when useful; preserve its source/date and distinguish observed feedback from agent interpretation.
- Decide accepted business limitations and authorize the release explicitly. Manager task acceptance, automated tests and a completed prototype do not constitute human acceptance.
- Assign operational responsibilities and review actual outcomes/feedback after release; decide changed priorities through the normal planning loop.

Record these in brief.md, decisions.md, testing/uat.md and the release/operations records as applicable. Reuse existing answers and authority; do not add a mandatory approval round for every task, preflight or minor technical choice. Ask only for unresolved decisions that affect the work, using the existing human-decision handoff when needed.
